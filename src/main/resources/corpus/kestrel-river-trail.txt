# Kestrel River Trail Guide
## 1. The route at a glance
The Kestrel River Trail runs for 62 kilometres from Alder Bridge to the harbour town of Port Merrow,
following the river from its upland valley to the sea. Most people walk it in four days. The total ascent
is 1,850 metres and the highest point is the Corvin Saddle at 1,240 metres. The whole route is waymarked
with a blue kingfisher symbol on posts and rocks.

The trail is a walking route. Bicycles are not allowed on the section inside the Upper Kestrel Reserve.

## 2. When to go
The best time to walk the trail is from the middle of May to the end of September. Snow often lies on the
Corvin Saddle until early May. In April the Hollow Ford crossing can be impassable after heavy rain.

July is the warmest month but also the worst for midges, which are thickest in the marsh near the coast.
In October the days are short and the mountain hut is closed.
@page
## 3. Stage by stage
Stage 1 goes from Alder Bridge to Fenwick Mill. It is 14 kilometres long and stays close to the river on
a level path through meadows, which makes it an easy first day.

Stage 2 goes from Fenwick Mill to Corvin Hut. It is 17 kilometres long with 900 metres of climbing, most
of it in the last five kilometres. This is the hardest stage of the trail and should be started early.

Stage 3 goes from Corvin Hut to Stonecross. It is 16 kilometres long, crosses the Corvin Saddle in the
first hour and then descends steadily through pine forest.

Stage 4 goes from Stonecross to Port Merrow. It is 15 kilometres long and crosses the estuary marsh on a
raised boardwalk before reaching the harbour.

Walkers with less time often skip Stage 1 by taking the morning bus to Fenwick Mill.
@page
## 4. Permits and fees
A camping permit is needed to stay overnight in the Upper Kestrel Reserve, which covers the trail between
Fenwick Mill and Stonecross. The permit costs 8 crowns per person per night and can be bought online or
at the ranger station in Fenwick Mill. Groups are limited to eight people. Day walkers do not need a
permit.

Dogs must be kept on a lead between 1 March and 31 July to protect ground-nesting birds. Open fires are
prohibited everywhere on the trail; only gas or spirit stoves may be used.

## 5. Accommodation
Corvin Hut has 24 bunks and a simple kitchen. In July and August a bunk must be reserved at least three
weeks ahead. The hut does not accept card payments, so bring cash.

Fenwick Mill has an inn with twelve rooms. The campsite at Stonecross has hot showers and a drying room.
Wild camping is permitted only above 600 metres and at least 100 metres away from the river.
@page
## 6. Water and food
Water taken from the river must be filtered or boiled, because cattle graze upstream. There are reliable
springs with drinking water at kilometre 9, at kilometre 23 just below Corvin Hut, and at kilometre 41.

There are no shops between Fenwick Mill and Stonecross, so carry food for two days on that section. The
shop in Stonecross is closed on Sundays.

## 7. Safety
There is no mobile phone coverage between kilometre 20 and kilometre 38. An emergency shelter with a
radio telephone stands at kilometre 31. The number for mountain rescue is 114.

Do not cross the Hollow Ford if the water is above knee height. The high-water alternative uses the
Tanner footbridge upstream and adds five kilometres to Stage 1. Weather on the saddle changes quickly, so
carry a waterproof jacket and a warm layer even in summer.
@page
## 8. Getting there and back
Trains run every hour from Marlow Junction to Alder Bridge and take 50 minutes. Parking at Alder Bridge
station costs 4 crowns per day.

From Port Merrow, bus 47 returns to Alder Bridge three times a day. The last bus leaves at 17:40 and the
journey takes one hour and 35 minutes. On Sundays there are only two buses.

## 9. Wildlife and conduct
Kingfishers are seen along the whole river, and otters are sometimes seen at dawn near Fenwick Mill. A
viewing platform with a fixed telescope at kilometre 52 overlooks an osprey nest that has been occupied
every summer since 2016.

Take all litter with you, keep to the marked path in the reserve and close every gate behind you.
