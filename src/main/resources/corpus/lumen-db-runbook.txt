# Lumen Database Operations Runbook
## 1. Cluster overview
The Lumen production cluster consists of one primary and two replicas spread over three availability
zones. The nodes are called lumen-a, lumen-b and lumen-c, and lumen-a is normally the primary. Replication
to one replica is synchronous and to the other asynchronous, so a committed transaction always exists on
at least two nodes.

Applications never connect to a node directly. They connect to the connection proxy on port 6432, which
routes them to the current primary. The database itself listens on port 5433 and that port is reachable
only from the proxy and from the bastion host.

## 2. On-call basics
The on-call rotation changes every Tuesday at 10:00 UTC. Alerts are delivered through Beacon and must be
acknowledged within five minutes. A severity 1 alert that is not acknowledged within 20 minutes
escalates to the engineering manager.

Write down every action taken during an incident in the incident channel as it happens. The timeline is
needed for the review afterwards.
@page
## 3. Backups
A full base backup is taken every night at 01:30 UTC. Write-ahead log segments are archived continuously
to the bucket lumen-wal-archive, which allows point-in-time recovery to any second inside the retention
period.

Daily backups are kept for 35 days and one backup per month is kept for 12 months. A restore test is
carried out on the first Monday of every month. The most recent test restored the 310 GB database in 47
minutes.

## 4. Monitoring thresholds
Replication lag raises a warning at 30 seconds and pages the on-call engineer at 120 seconds. Disk usage
raises a warning at 75 percent and pages at 88 percent.

The cluster accepts at most 500 connections, and an alert pages when 450 are in use. Transactions that
have been open for more than 15 minutes are flagged on the dashboard, because they hold back vacuum and
cause table bloat.
@page
## 5. Planned failover
A planned failover, also called a switchover, moves the primary role to a replica without losing data.
Announce it in the db-ops channel 30 minutes beforehand. Check that the replication lag of the target is
below five seconds.

Run the command lumenctl switchover --target lumen-b from the bastion host. Writes are paused for
roughly eight seconds while the roles change. The proxy re-points to the new primary by itself, which
takes about 20 seconds. Finally confirm that the old primary has rejoined the cluster as a replica.

## 6. Unplanned failover
If the primary misses heartbeats for 30 seconds, the cluster manager promotes the synchronous replica
automatically. Do not promote a node by hand unless the automatic failover has not happened after two
minutes.

After an unplanned failover the old primary may contain transactions that were never replicated. It must
be rebuilt with lumenctl reinit before it rejoins. Never simply restart it, because two nodes that both
believe they are primary cause a split brain.
@page
## 7. Common incidents
Disk full. Delete old server logs under /var/log/lumen first; logs older than seven days are safe to
remove. If that is not enough, grow the volume in steps of 20 percent. Never delete files from the pg_wal
directory by hand, because the database cannot start without them.

Connection exhaustion. Find the service that holds the most idle connections on the proxy dashboard. Each
service has a pool of 40 connections. Restarting the proxy drops every client connection and is the last
resort.

Slow queries. Statements slower than 500 milliseconds are written to the slow query log. Look for a
missing index before anything else. A query that has been running for more than 30 minutes may be
cancelled once its owner has confirmed.
@page
## 8. Maintenance windows
The weekly maintenance window is on Sunday from 02:00 to 04:00 UTC. Minor version upgrades are applied
to the replicas first and are completed with a switchover. A major version upgrade needs a change request
that has been approved at least 10 working days in advance.

Operations that lock whole tables, such as a full vacuum, are forbidden outside the maintenance window.

## 9. Access and credentials
Production access goes through the bastion host bastion.lumen.internal with short-lived certificates that
are valid for eight hours. Nobody holds a permanent database password.

The break-glass account is stored in the vault under ops/lumen/breakglass. Every use of it triggers an
audit review within two working days. Service passwords are rotated every 90 days by an automated job.
